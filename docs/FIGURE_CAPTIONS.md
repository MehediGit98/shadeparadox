# Figure captions

Figure 1. Model office geometry: (a) section showing the aperture and seated-eye height; (b) plan showing the three E1 depths. The illustrated 50% shade coverage and light paths are schematic. E3 uses the 2.5 m depth and 12 world headings; the default E1 view is 45° from the outward façade normal.

Figure 2. E1 comparisons with always-open blinds: (a) mean mEDI retention, with the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles from resampling the 12 orientation–depth cells; (b) changes in occupied-time fractions. Hatched bars identify the stochastic sensitivity case at intercept −8. Intervals describe resampled grid means and are not population confidence limits; the intercept sweep is shown separately in Supplementary Figure S1.

Figure 3. E3 heading-dependent (a) mean mEDI and (b) mean shade coverage. World headings are clockwise from north; 270° faces the west-facing window. Line styles and markers distinguish the rules. Lines join discrete 30° samples and do not imply continuous variation. Values average five seeds and the weighted representative-day sequence; always-open coverage is zero.

Figure 4. E2 diagnostic: (a) identical mean mEDI across six encodings and four optimisers; (b) zero between-encoding and between-optimiser dispersions. All selections kept the blinds open; electric lighting could still operate. Constant colour is intentional. One distinct selected outcome makes the ratio 0/0 undefined.

Figure S1. Sensitivity of mean E1 mEDI retention to the assumed stochastic closing intercept, with slope fixed at 1.6. Values summarise the 12 orientation–depth cells and five behavioural seeds; controller state persists across the representative dates. The dashed line identifies the default intercept −8. The connected samples describe an illustrative model sensitivity and do not estimate an occupant-population relationship.
